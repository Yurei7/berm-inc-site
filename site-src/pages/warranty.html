Behind the Walls

Protection Begins Before Construction

A Warranty Is Important. Prevention Is Better.

A warranty is important. But at Berm, we believe homeowner protection should begin much earlier — during design coordination, material selection, and construction itself.

Many of the details that determine how a home performs will eventually be hidden behind walls, ceilings, and finishes. That is why we pay particular attention to the building systems you may never see once your home is complete.

Our Approach to Building Better

We Look at the Home as One Complete System.

At Berm, building science influences the way we approach construction. Rather than looking at insulation, windows, ventilation, waterproofing, and structure as separate components, we consider how they interact with one another. Our focus includes:

  • Continuous Insulation — reducing unnecessary heat loss through the building enclosure.
  • Airtight Construction — careful detailing of the air barrier and its transitions throughout the home.
  • Thermal-Bridge Reduction — identifying locations where heat can bypass the insulation layer and addressing them through better detailing.
  • High-Performance Windows & Doors — considering not only the performance of the product, but also how it connects to the surrounding wall assembly.
  • Moisture Management — managing rainwater, vapour, and drainage to support a more durable building enclosure.
  • Ventilation & Indoor Air Quality — recognizing that a well-performing home requires intentional ventilation and fresh-air management.

The Connections

Details Matter

Especially the Ones You Can't See.

Once drywall and finishes are installed, much of the building envelope disappears from view. Before that happens, Berm pays particular attention to critical areas such as:

  • Air-barrier continuity
  • Insulation transitions
  • Window and door installation
  • Flashing and water management
  • Foundation-to-wall connections
  • Roof-to-wall connections
  • Mechanical and electrical penetrations
  • Exterior-envelope transitions
  • Areas susceptible to thermal bridging

These details may be hidden when the home is finished, but their performance can affect comfort and durability for years.
